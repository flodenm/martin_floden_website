# Comparing GDP across countries

India had a lower price level than the United States: after conversion into dollars, the Indian price level was 30.3 percent of the U.S. price level.

- **Part a.** Convert Indian GDP into dollars using the market exchange rate. What was the ratio of Indian GDP to U.S. GDP according to this calculation?

# Has Finland fallen further behind?

In Lecture Notes 1, we showed that GDP per capita can be written as $$\frac{Y}{N}
    =
    \frac{Y}{H}
    \times
    \frac{H}{E}
    \times
    \frac{E}{N^{wa}}
    \times
    \frac{N^{wa}}{N}.$$

For a variable $X$, define its annualized percentage growth rate between 2000 and 2025 as $$g_X
    =
    100\left[
        \left(\frac{X_{2025}}{X_{2000}}\right)^{1/25}-1
    \right].$$

We can then use the approximation $$g_{Y/N}
    \approx
    g_{Y/H}
    +g_{H/E}
    +g_{E/N^{wa}}
    +g_{N^{wa}/N}.$$

# What explains growth in output per hour?

The decomposition in the previous question treated output per hour, $Y/H$, as an observed component of GDP per capita. We now use growth accounting to examine why output per hour changed.

Suppose that production is described by $$Y_t=A_tK_t^\alpha H_t^{1-\alpha},$$ where $H_t$ is total hours worked. Dividing by hours gives $$\frac{Y_t}{H_t}
    =
    A_t\left(\frac{K_t}{H_t}\right)^\alpha.$$

The table reports capital per hour in Finland, Sweden, and the United States. For each country, capital per hour is expressed as a percentage of capital per hour in the United States in 2000. This normalization does not affect the calculated growth rates.

|:--------------|------------------------:|------:|
| Finland       |                   110.4 | 174.0 |
| Sweden        |                   106.4 | 163.7 |
| United States |                   100.0 | 156.9 |

Source: AMECO, Penn World Table 11.0, and own calculations. The latest available PWT observations, from 2023, are used as proxies for 2025.

Assume that the capital share is $\alpha=1/3$.

1.  **Part a.** Calculate $g_{K/H}$, the annualized growth rate of capital per hour, in Finland, Sweden, and the United States between 2000 and 2025. Compare the rates of capital deepening across the three countries.

2.  **Part b.** Growth accounting gives the approximation $$g_{Y/H}
                \approx
                g_A+\alpha g_{K/H}.$$ Using your estimates of $g_{Y/H}$ from the previous question, decompose productivity growth in each country into a contribution from capital deepening and a contribution from TFP growth.

3.  **Part c.** Does this calculation show that differences in technological progress caused the differences in productivity growth? Explain what else may be captured by measured TFP growth.

# Two changes in the labor force in the Solow model

Consider the Solow model with $$Y_t=AK_t^\alpha L_t^{1-\alpha},
    \qquad
    K_{t+1}=(1-\delta)K_t+sY_t.$$ The economy initially is in a steady state. Throughout the question, $A$, $s$, $\delta$, and $\alpha$ are constant.

## A one-time increase in the level of the labor force

At date $t=0$, the labor force rises immediately and permanently from $L$ to $L'>L$. After this one-time change, the labor force remains constant at $L'$.

- **Part a.** What happens immediately to aggregate capital $K_0$, capital per worker $k_0$, aggregate output $Y_0$, and output per worker $y_0$? Distinguish carefully between quantities that are predetermined and quantities that can change immediately.

- **Part b.** Draw the Solow diagram in per-worker terms. Does either the investment curve $sAk^\alpha$ or the depreciation line $\delta k$ shift when the labor force increases?

- **Part c.** Describe the transition after the increase in the labor force. What happens to the growth rate of output per worker during the transition?

- **Part d.** Compare the initial and final steady states. What happens in the long run to capital and output per worker? What happens to aggregate capital and aggregate output?

## A higher population growth rate

Now return to the initial steady state and instead suppose that the growth rate of the labor force rises permanently from $n$ to $n'>n$.

- **Part e.** Draw the Solow diagram with population growth. Which curve shifts when $n$ rises?

- **Part f.** What happens during the transition to capital and output per worker? What are the long-run effects on their levels and growth rates?

- **Part g.** What is the long-run growth rate of aggregate output before and after the change?

- **Part h.** Summarize the key difference between a one-time increase in the *level* of the labor force and a permanent increase in its *growth rate*.
