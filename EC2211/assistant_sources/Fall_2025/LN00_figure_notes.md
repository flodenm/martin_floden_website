# Lecture 0 figure notes — Fall 2025 archive

These notes describe instructional figures referenced by
`LN00_Course_Introduction.tex`. The image files are not assistant sources.
Descriptions report only what is visible in the archived figures and their
captions; they are not current-data claims.

## What is GDP and what does it measure?

The chart shows Swedish GDP at current prices from 1970 through 2024, measured
in SEK billions. Nominal GDP rises from about SEK 196 billion in 1970 to about
SEK 6,392 billion in 2024. The purpose is to motivate the question of how much
of the rise reflects larger quantities of output and how much reflects higher
prices; the chart alone does not measure real growth.

## Why do some countries grow rapidly, others not?

The chart plots log real GDP per capita over a very long historical period for
Sweden, the United Kingdom, Korea, and Kenya. Sweden and the United Kingdom show
large long-run increases; Korea shows rapid catch-up in the later twentieth
century; Kenya's increase is smaller. The comparison motivates the study of
large cross-country differences in levels and growth rates.

## Why did inflation increase so much?

The chart plots annual inflation from 2000 to 2025 for Sweden, the euro area,
and the United States. Inflation is mostly low or moderate before the pandemic,
then rises sharply and broadly in 2021–2023 before declining. It illustrates a
shared inflation surge, not a causal explanation of that surge.

## What can central banks achieve?

The archived image is a 2025 news headline stating that the Federal Reserve was
set to lower interest rates despite rising inflation. It introduces the tension
between inflation stabilization and other central-bank objectives. The image
does not by itself establish the reasons for the decision or a general policy
rule.

## Is it important that central banks are independent?

The archived image is a news headline dated 26 August 2025 reporting, with the
word “fires” in quotation marks, an attempt by President Trump to remove Federal
Reserve governor Lisa Cook. It is used to introduce political pressure on a
central bank and the question of central-bank independence. Treat this only as
a description of the archived headline, not as current guidance or a complete
account of the event.

## Should we worry about high and growing public debt?

The chart plots general-government debt as a share of GDP for France, the
United Kingdom, and the United States from 2000 to 2025. Debt ratios generally
rise, with visible jumps around the global financial crisis and the pandemic.
The United States has the highest ratio late in the sample, followed by France
and then the United Kingdom. The figure motivates a policy question; it does
not itself determine whether a given debt level is sustainable.

## Modeling principles

The diagram shows inputs on the left — parameters and exogenous variables —
entering a model, which produces endogenous variables or outcomes on the
right. It illustrates that a model maps specified external inputs into
internally determined results.
